Neighborhood GuideSavannah, GA

Starland

Starland pulses with creative energy, colorful murals, independent cafés, and a grid of bungalows that give Savannah's beloved arts district its unmistakable soul.

A Canvas in the Heart of Savannah

Starland sits just south of Forsyth Park in Savannah, Georgia, occupying a compact, walkable grid that has quietly evolved into one of the city's most spirited creative corridors. Bounded roughly by Anderson, Bull, 34th, and Habersham streets, the district takes its name from the Starland Dairy that once anchored the area — a piece of industrial history that gave way to a neighborhood now known for its independent spirit and eclectic streetscape.

Architecture and Housing Character

The residential blocks of Starland are lined primarily with early-to-mid twentieth-century craftsman bungalows, shotgun cottages, and modest Victorian-era homes. Front porches face tree-canopied sidewalks, and painted wood siding in a wide range of hues gives the streets a vivid, handcrafted quality. The scale throughout is intimate — two- and one-story structures predominate, keeping the neighborhood feeling distinctly human and unhurried even as the surrounding city hums with activity.

Arts, Culture, and Local Life

Starland is perhaps best recognized for the murals that appear on building ends, fences, and tucked-away walls throughout the district. These works shift and grow over time, making the neighborhood feel like an open-air gallery that rewards repeat visits. Independent coffee shops, vintage boutiques, recording studios, and working artist studios share blocks with yoga spaces and plant shops, producing a streetscape that is equal parts productive and leisurely. The Starland Yard, an adaptive reuse complex built around vintage shipping containers and open-air gathering space, has become a gathering point for food trucks, local makers markets, and live music.

Walkability and Getting Around

One of Starland's most practical qualities is its walkability. The flat Savannah grid means residents can reach Forsyth Park — with its famous fountain, open lawns, and weekend farmers market — in minutes on foot. The broader Victorian District and the shops along Bull Street corridor are similarly close. Cyclists find the terrain easy, and the neighborhood's position between downtown and the Midtown area makes it a convenient home base for exploring the wider city.

Pace and Atmosphere

The pace in Starland is decidedly unhurried without feeling sleepy. Weekend mornings bring neighbors to sidewalk café tables and the sound of live music warming up a block away. Weekday afternoons carry the quieter rhythm of small studios and independent businesses at work. The neighborhood rewards those who enjoy discovering things slowly — a new mural around a corner, a pop-up market in a parking lot, a long conversation over a locally roasted cup of coffee.
