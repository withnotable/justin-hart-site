Tybee Island

Tybee Island brings coastal Georgia's laid-back beach town spirit to life, just 18 miles east of Savannah along the Atlantic shore.

A Genuine Georgia Beach Town

Tybee Island sits at the eastern tip of Chatham County, where the Savannah River meets the Atlantic Ocean. Reached by a scenic causeway that winds through salt marshes, tidal creeks, and barrier island estuary, Tybee carries the unhurried rhythm of a place shaped entirely by the sea. The island is small — roughly two and a half miles long and less than a mile wide — yet it holds a distinct identity that has drawn visitors and residents for well over a century.

History and Architecture

Tybee's history reaches back to colonial Georgia, when the island served as a navigational landmark and later a strategic military outpost. The Tybee Island Light Station, one of the oldest and tallest lighthouses in the Southeast, has guided mariners since the early 1700s and remains a working lighthouse today. The island's built environment reflects its layered past: classic coastal cottages with wide porches and weathered shingles sit alongside mid-century bungalows, beach houses on stilts, and a modest collection of local shops and restaurants clustered near the pier and pavilion. Large resort developments are notably absent, preserving the small-town scale that defines the island's character.

Beaches, Parks, and the Outdoors

Tybee's beaches stretch along the Atlantic and face the wide Georgia coast, offering both broad sandy shores and quieter stretches near the north and south ends of the island. The Back River and estuary sides provide calm water access popular with kayakers and paddleboarders. Tybee Island Marine Science Center gives visitors and residents a window into the local coastal ecosystem, while the nearby Oatland Island Wildlife Center on the mainland adds to the region's natural attractions. The interplay of ocean, tidal marsh, and river makes outdoor life here genuinely varied.

Dining, Shopping, and Local Culture

The commercial heart of Tybee clusters near Tybrisa Street and the pier, where seafood restaurants, casual beach bars, ice cream shops, and locally owned boutiques define the commercial strip. The pace is deliberately relaxed — the island actively resists the chain-heavy development common to other coastal destinations. A rotating calendar of community events, including the Tybee Island Pirate Fest and various arts and music gatherings, reflects the island's creative and eccentric community spirit. Tybee is the kind of place where flip-flops are acceptable attire year-round.

Getting Around and Proximity

Highway 80 is the sole road connecting Tybee Island to the mainland, making the causeway drive — roughly 30 minutes from downtown Savannah — part of the daily rhythm for those who commute or venture into the city for work or culture. On the island itself, distances are short enough that bicycles and golf carts serve as practical and popular transportation. The compact geography means the beach, restaurants, and the lighthouse are rarely more than a short ride from anywhere on the island.
